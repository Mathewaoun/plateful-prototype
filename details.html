      Photo in, calories out, ingredient by ingredient
      
  
      SEG 4505 prototype for the Digital Health Inc. food journal. It runs the RFB's measurement method end to end: detect each visible ingredient, auto-calibrate scale, estimate volume, convert to weight with density tables, then to calories with nutrition tables.
      

  
      Pipeline (lights up as it runs)
      
  
        
    
      1. Capture and upload
        Photo resized on-device, sent to the cloud. Production: pre-signed upload to Amazon S3.
        2. 
    
      2. AI ingredient detection
        Every visible ingredient gets its own box, not just "salad". Prototype uses a vision model; production uses a segmentation model on Amazon SageMaker.
        3. 
    
      3. Auto-calibration
        Real-world scale estimated from the scene itself, with no reference object (RFB §6). Gives cm per pixel.
        4. 
    
      4. Area → volume
        Area = box × coverage × cm². Volume = area × depth, assuming the surface continues down uniformly (RFB §4).
        5. 
    
      5. Volume → weight → kcal
        Density table gives grams; nutrition table gives kcal per 100 g. Total = sum over ingredients (RFB §5).
        6. 
    
      6. User correction → incremental training
        Edits are queued as labelled samples to fine-tune the model incrementally, not retrain from scratch (RFB §9).
        7.

      Alternatives we evaluated
      
  
      
    
      DecisionOptions consideredChosen and why
      
    
      
      Logging methodManual entry; barcode scan; photo reviewed by a human (MealSnap-style)Automatic photo analysis (VBM): the client's preferred method; no human in the loop, so it scales to millions of photos
      
      
      RecognitionWhole-dish classification; multi-label tagsIngredient-level detection + segmentation: only option meeting FR1 ("carrots, lettuce, tomato", not "salad") and giving areas for weight
      
      
      ScaleReference object (coin, thumb); depth sensor (LiDAR, only some phones)Single-image auto-calibration [3]: reference objects are forbidden, LiDAR excludes most Android phones
      
      
      Where AI runsOn the phoneCloud (AWS): higher accuracy (94.33 % vs 87.16 % local [6]), model updates without app releases; offline photos are queued (H13)
      
      
      TrainingFull retraining on all dataIncremental fine-tuning on new labelled photos + a replay sample of old data: required by the RFB, far cheaper
      
      
      MobileTwo native apps (Swift + Kotlin)One cross-platform codebase (Flutter): one team of 19 ships both platforms; juniors learn one stack
